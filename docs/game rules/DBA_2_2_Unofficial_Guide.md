## Introduction

How simple can a set of miniatures rules be and still hold your interest over time? How short can a game be and still give you the satisfaction of a good contest? How few figures can you put on the table and still feel like a general?

The experience of a large group of gamers over the last twenty years has shown that the rules can be pretty darn simple, the games very quick, and the armies downright diminutive. Simple enough for new gamers to learn enough to get started in five minutes and feel a reasonable mastery after two or three games. But with enough depth of play to keep players involved for more than a decade. Quick enough to get three or more games in over the space of an evening. And armies small enough for many gamers to collect dozens.

*De Bellis Antiquitatis* (a.k.a. DBA) broke onto the miniature wargaming scene in 1990 and the community of dedicated followers has grown steadily since then. The ideas were new, the game mechanics worked, and it filled a big empty space in the wargaming world.

Unfortunately, the spread of DBA has always been hampered by a cryptic writing style and its lack of clear diagrams and examples. In addition, the ambiguity of the language used has meant that a common understanding of what the rules really mean has been elusive.

*The Unofficial Guide to DBA* attempts to tackle both of these problems. First, the authors hope that newcomers to DBA will find it a bit easier to jump in. While there is no substitute for sitting down and playing a game with an experienced player, having an accessible explanation available can only serve to broaden the reach of DBA.

Second, we also hope to capture what we see as the mainstream of how DBA is actually played. It is written by a group of active players and written with the community of active players in mind. If it makes it a little easier for gamers with different backgrounds to play with a common understanding of the rules, then we have been successful in our aim.

### v2.2+

In September 2012, a group of DBA tournament organizers released the v2.2+ amendments for DBA. The amendments provide a number of improvements to DBA 2.2, while maintaining the overall feel of play and maintaining consistency with DBA 2.2 armies. Since that time, v2.2+ has seen widespread adoption and is being played worldwide.

> *The Unofficial Guide to DBA* is based on the DBA version 2.2 rulebook with the v2.2+ amendments applied. All explanations presented here are consistent with the text of that rulebook as amended. The Big Battle DBA, Giant DBA, and campaign rules are not covered.
>
> See Print and Online Resources for information on purchasing a copy of the DBA 2.2 rulebook and downloading the v2.2+ amendments.

## Game Materials

What do you need in order to play? The basics are:

- Two players
- A game board
- An army for each player
- A six-sided die for each player
- A measuring device
- A number of terrain pieces

Kibitzers, snacks, and a tasty beverage are optional.

### Game Board

The game board is a square surface, typically made of cloth, felt, or wood. All action takes place on the game board. The standard game board is **30" by 30"** for 15mm figures and **48" by 48"** for 25mm figures.

> While the standard game board is now 30" by 30" for 15mm figures, some gamers prefer to continue playing on the original 24" by 24" board. The smaller board may be more convenient in some circumstances, but comes at the cost of more "edge-of-the-world" affects.

### Armies

Each army consists of twelve troop elements, an optional camp, and an optional Camp Followers element. One of the twelve troop elements must be designated as the army's **general**.

An **element** is the basic building block of your army. It represents a group of soldiers (or camp followers) that move and fight as a single unit. Physically, an element consists of a rectangular base with one or more figures or models attached.

### Dice

DBA uses normal six-sided dice.

### Measuring Devices

- **Move Stick.** A small stick marked in 1 MU increments.
- **ZOC Marker.** A square piece of wood or metal, one base width on a side, used to determine if an element is within the ZOC of another element.

### Terrain Pieces

The surface of the game board is by default clear and open terrain. Possible terrain types include: Woods, Gentle Hill, Steep Hill, Marsh, Rough, Waterway, River, Road.

## Playing Scale

### Figure Scale

DBA is played in two basic scales: **15mm** figures and **25mm** figures. The two differ in element sizes and the ground scale, but the rules are the same.

### Ground Scale

Most distances are specified in **movement units (MU)**.

- 1 MU = ½ base width
- 15mm figures: 1 MU = 20mm
- 25mm figures: 1 MU = 30mm

Players may measure distances at any time during the game. Pre-measuring is okay.

> DBA will work with figures of any scale. Because the number of figures actually mounted on the base has no affect on the game mechanics, you're free to substitute any scale figure. In practice, 25mm is commonly used as a shorthand for all figures of roughly the same size, including 20mm plastic and 28mm+ figures.

### Time and Numbers

Nominally, each turn in DBA simulates approximately 15 minutes of time on the battlefield and each figure represents somewhere around 250 to 300 men. Given that DBA is a highly abstract game, it's best not to place much emphasis on these numbers.

## Elements

Every element has a **front edge**, two **side edges**, and a **rear edge**, plus two **front corners** and two **rear corners**.

- 15mm figures: standard element width = 40mm
- 25mm figures: standard element width = 60mm

The depth depends on the element type and subtype.

### Element Categories

There are two broad categories: **Mounted** and **Foot**.

### Element Types

There are twenty-four different element types:

- **Elephants** — Big and clumsy. Dangerous to enemy and friend alike.
- **Knights** — Big horses. Lots of armor. Not known for restraint.
- **Heavy Chariots** — Knights with wheels.
- **Cataphracts** — Armored men on armored horses. Slow but very tough.
- **Cavalry** — Ordinary mounted. More cautious than Knights, less flighty than Light Horse.
- **Light Chariots** — Cavalry with wheels.
- **Scythed Chariots** — Freaking Psychotic Cuisinart Scythed Chariots of DOOM!
- **Camelry** — They smell bad and they spit a lot.
- **Light Horse** — Quick and agile. Float like a butterfly, sting like a butterfly.
- **Light Camelry** — Float like a butterfly, sting like a butterfly. A butterfly that likes sand.
- **Blades** — Well armed and well armored, top dog of the foot troops.
- **Raiders** — The top dog's feisty little brother.
- **Spears** — Big shields and long pointy sticks.
- **Pikes** — Like a big porcupine in a bad mood.
- **Light Spears** — The big porcupine's prickly little brother.
- **Bows** — Bows, crossbows, and longbows en masse.
- **Pavises** — Bowmen with the good sense to stand behind men with shields and spears.
- **Warband** — Heavy on the woad, light on the discipline.
- **Auxilia** — Good in the bad.
- **Psiloi** — Your basic skirmishers. Catch 'em if you can.
- **Hordes** — They don't want to be there. Really.
- **Artillery** — Some lob big rocks, some shoot big arrows. Don't get hit.
- **War Wagons** — Big slow wagons. Like a fortress on wheels.
- **Camp Followers** — Tramps and harlots. Our kind of people.

## Army Lists

Army lists designate what types of elements are allowed in an army. The DBA rulebook contains 310 army lists, covering armies from Ancient Sumer to Late Medieval Burgundy.

### Reading an Army List

Each army list includes:

- **Book/Army Number** — Unique identifier derived from the corresponding DBM army list.
- **Army Name**
- **Start and End Dates**
- **List of Enemies** — Armies historically attested to have fought this army.
- **List of Allies** — Allies separated by "or" are mutually exclusive; by "and" must both be taken; by "and/or" may be taken individually or together.
- **Reference Book**
- **Home Topography**
- **Aggression** — Rating 0–4; used in determining the invader.
- **Element Types Allowed**

### Sublists

Some army lists provide two or more sublists, allowing tailoring to a specific time period, region, or state. Each sublist has its own start and end dates, enemies, and allies.

### Sample Army List

```
I/17. Hyksos. 1645BC-1537BC.
Littoral. Ag:2.
E = (a) I/2a, 6a, 7a, 15. (b) I/2a, 6a, 7a, 20b, 22a.
A = (a) 1/2ab. (b) I/2b. Ref: AANE.
(a) 1645-1591BC: 1xLCh (Gen), 4x3Bd, 1x4Ax, 4x3Ax, 2x2Ps.
(b) 1590-1537BC: 1xLCh (Gen), 3xLCh, 4x3Bd, 1x4Ax, 2x2Ps, 1x7Hd or 2Ps.
```

- An entry `4x3Bd` means four elements of 3Bd.
- `(Gen)` means that element may be designated as the general.
- `1x7Hd or 2Ps` means one element that may be either 7Hd or 2Ps.
- `2x3/4Ax` means two elements in any combination of 3Ax or 4Ax.
- `4x3Kn//4Bd` means four elements of 3Kn that may dismount as 4Bd.
- The total always comes to **twelve elements**.

## Terrain Pieces

### Area Terrain Pieces

A legal area terrain piece must:

- Be roughly oval (except Rough in Arable, which may be oval, rectangular, or trapezoidal).
- Be at least 2 MU across in all directions.
- Fit inside an imaginary rectangle whose length plus width is no greater than **18 MU**.
- Woods, gentle hills, steep hills, dunes, and oases may not be longer than twice their width.
- Gentle hills and steep hills must slope up to a crest line (important for line-of-sight).

### Linear Terrain Pieces

A legal **waterway** must:
- Be between 1 and 6 MU in depth.
- No more than half may be greater than 3 MU in depth.

A legal **river or road** must:
- Not be more than 2 MU across.
- Not be longer than 1½ times the distance between its ends.

### Terrain Table

| Category | Terrain Type | Class |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| Area | Woods | Bad Going |
| Area | Gentle Hill | Good Going |
| Area | Steep Hill | Bad Going |
| Area | Marsh | Bad Going |
| Area | Rough | Bad Going |
| Area | Dunes | Bad Going* |
| Area | Oasis | Bad Going* |
| Linear | Waterway | Impassable |
| Linear | River | Special |
| Linear | Road | Special |

\* Camelry and Light Camelry treat Dunes and Oasis as Good Going.

### Roads

Roads affect movement (road moves, crossing a river, second and subsequent movement). For all other purposes, treat a road as the underlying terrain type.

## Camps

Every army must have a camp **unless** the army has more than one element of War Wagons.

A camp may be occupied by one and only one element, or it may be unoccupied. An element may not be partly in and partly out of a camp.

### Shape and Size

A camp may be of any shape. It must:
- Be large enough to hold a troop or camp follower element.
- Fit inside an imaginary rectangle whose length plus width is no greater than **8 MU**.

### Camp Followers

Each army that has a camp may also have a camp follower element (optional). A camp follower element must begin the game occupying its camp and may not leave the camp as a tactical move. The camp follower element does not count against the twelve elements allowed.

If the camp follower element is not used, the empty camp is still treated as if occupied by camp followers, unless the player notes otherwise.

---

## Rivers

Rivers represent medium-sized rivers that may be forded with difficulty (e.g., the Granicus, 334 BC). Really big rivers (Nile, Rhine) are waterways and are impassable.

### Crossing a River on a Road

Elements crossing a river while moving on a road ignore all river affects on movement.

### Entering a River Off Road

An element entering a river off road must do so while facing within 45 degrees of perpendicular to the river. Once any part of an element is in a river, it may only:
- Move directly forward or directly backward
- Move to contact or line up with an enemy element
- Pivot to maintain a legal alignment with the river

Elements may only start in or move into a river if moving as single elements or as part of a column.

### Movement Distance in a River

A single element or column whose front edge starts in or enters a river may only move **2 MU** during that tactical move.

### Defending the Bank

An element defending the bank of a river receives **+1** in close combat if:
- No part of the element is in the river
- The element is not on a road crossing the river
- The element is facing the river and its entire front edge is within 1 MU of the river
- The enemy element is at least partly in the river

> **Warning:** Rivers can substantially lengthen a game by slowing movement and encouraging defensive play.

## Setting Up a Game

Six steps:
1. Select Armies
2. Roll for Invader
3. Set Up Terrain
4. Roll for Board Orientation
5. Place Camps
6. Deploy Armies

### Rolling for Invader

Each player rolls one die and adds their army's aggression rating. Higher total = **invader**. Lower total = **defender**. Re-roll ties.

### Rolling for Board Orientation

The invader designates any three edges as 1, 2, and 3; the remaining edge is 4–5–6. Roll one die to determine the invader's base edge.

### Placing Camps

Defender places camp first, then invader. The camp must be placed in contact with the army's base edge (or waterway if one runs along the base edge). Camps must be placed in good going.

### Deploying Armies

1. Defender places army
2. Invader places army
3. Defender may swap up to two pairs of elements

Elements must be placed within **10 MU** of the army's base edge and at least **4 MU** from the side edges.

Armies with Littoral home topography may hold back 0–4 elements for a littoral landing. Elements allowed to dismount may be deployed as either their mounted or foot element type.

## Terrain Placement

### Home Topography

Determines what terrain types the defender may place.

### Terrain Placement: Defender

The defender chooses terrain pieces from those allowed for their home topography and places them on the board.

### Terrain Placement: Invader

If the game board has no bad going within 8 MU of the center, the invader rolls one die. On a 1 or 2, the invader may move the smallest bad going terrain piece to within 8 MU of the center, or choose and place a new bad going terrain piece (no larger than 4 MU × 4 MU) within 8 MU of the center.

The invader then rolls a die for each area terrain piece. On a 1 or 2, the invader may shift the terrain piece up to 4 MU in any direction or rotate it up to 45 degrees.

### Terrain Placement Restrictions

A legal game board must:
- Have between **3 and 5** terrain pieces
- Have between 1 and 3 compulsory terrain pieces
- Have between 0 and 3 optional terrain pieces
- Have at least 3 quarters containing some part of a terrain piece
- Have at least 2 quarters containing some part of a waterway, river, or bad going piece
- Have no more than one each of waterway, river, or oasis
- Have no more than 3 of any compulsory type and no more than 2 of any optional type
- Have area terrain pieces at least one base width apart

Additional restrictions: Road must run from one edge to either a different edge or another road. Waterway must be flush with one edge, extending its full length. River must run from one edge to the opposite edge (or from waterway to opposite edge), placed at least 8 MU from the parallel edges.

### Topography Table

| Home Topography | Compulsory Terrain | Optional Terrain |
|-----------------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Arable | Road | River, Steep Hill, Gentle Hill, Woods, Waterway, Rough* |
| Forest | Woods | River, Marsh, Gentle Hill |
| Hilly | Steep Hill | River, Woods, Road |
| Steppe | Gentle Hill | River, Rough |
| Dry | Rough | Dunes, Steep Hill, Oasis |
| Tropical | Woods | River, Marsh, Rough, Road |
| Littoral | Waterway | Either Steep Hill or Marsh; either Woods or Dunes; River |

\* A Rough terrain piece in Arable represents ploughed fields.

### Overlaying Terrain Pieces

- A road may pass through any area terrain piece and may cross a river or another road.
- A river may not pass through a gentle or steep hill, but may pass through any other area terrain piece.
- Otherwise, no terrain piece may be placed on top of another.

## Dismounting

Dismounting is changing an element from a mounted type to a foot type, specified in army lists by double slashes (`//`). For example, `4x3Kn//4Bd` = four elements of 3Kn that may dismount as 4Bd.

- Dismounting may only occur during the deployment phase.
- An element placed as its foot type may **not** change back to its mounted type during the game.

---

## Sequence of Play

The invader goes first. Each turn:

1. **PIP Phase** — Roll one die for Player Initiative Points.
2. **Tactical Movement Phase** — Voluntary movement of the active player's elements.
3. **Distant Shooting Phase** — Both armies may shoot (Bows, Pavises, War Wagons, Artillery).
4. **Close Combat Phase** — Elements in full front contact with the enemy engage in combat.

---

## Tactical Movement

Tactical movement is voluntary; the player is not obligated to move elements. Tactical movement includes movement into contact; there is no separate charge move. Most elements may only make one tactical move per turn.

There are three types of tactical moves:

- **Single Element Move** — Move one element with great flexibility.
- **Group Move** — Move multiple elements with limited maneuver.
- **Littoral Landing** — Specialized move in the first turn.

---

## PIPs

Each tactical move requires spending at least **1 PIP**.

- A player may not spend more PIPs than were rolled.
- A player is not obligated to spend PIPs.
- PIPs may not be saved from one turn to the next.

Extra PIPs are not required in the player's first turn. In subsequent turns, extra PIPs are required:

- **Category 1** (+1 PIP): The move includes Elephants, Hordes, War Wagons, or Artillery.
- **Category 2** (+1 PIP): All of the moving element/group is outside the general's command distance, OR the general has been destroyed.
- **Category 3** (+1 PIP): The general's element is entirely in a camp, woods, oasis, or marsh (does not apply when making a single element move with the general's element).

Only apply one extra PIP per category.

### Command Distance

- **16 MU** if there is a clear line-of-sight between any part of the general's element and any part of the moving element.
- **8 MU** if the line-of-sight is blocked.

Line-of-sight is blocked by the crest of a hill, or the edge of a camp, woods, oasis, or dunes. Also blocked if the moving element is entirely inside a wood, oasis, or dune.

Measure command distance between the closest points of the general's element and the moving element or group.

## Movement Distance

An element is a **road move** if the entire move is along a road. An element is a **bad going move** if (off road) any part of the element enters bad going during the move. Otherwise it is a **good going move**.

### Movement Table

| Element Types | Good Going (MU) | Bad Going (MU) |
|--------------|-----------------|----------------|
| Light Horse, Light Camelry | 8 | 3 |
| Cavalry, Light Chariots | 6 | 3 |
| Scythed Chariots | 6 | 3 |
| Camelry | 5 | 3 |
| Knights, Cataphracts, Elephants | 4 | 3 |
| Auxilia | 5 | 5 |
| Psiloi, Light Spears | 4 | 4 |
| Raiders, Blades, Bows, Hordes, Spears, Pavises, Pikes, Warband | 3 | 3 |
| Artillery, War Wagons | 3 | None |
| Camp Followers | None | None |

Road movement distance: **8 MU** for Light Horse and Light Camelry; **6 MU** for all other types.

Artillery and War Wagon elements may not make a bad going move (except on a road).

Elements moving across a river may only move **2 MU** during that tactical move. A camp follower element may not make a tactical move.

### Measuring Movement Distance

Distance is measured using the element's front corner that moves the farthest, from its starting position to its ending position.

### Bad Going

An element may not treat part of its move as good going and part as bad going. If it enters bad going at any point, the entire move is a bad going move restricted to the bad going movement distance.

---

## Single Element Moves

In a single element move you move one element with great flexibility. Constraints:

- The element may not exceed its maximum movement distance.
- If the element starts in or enters an enemy ZOC, movement is limited.
- The element may not pass through a gap narrower than its leading edge.

A single element move must be taken as a series of intermediate moves if: a corner would pass through a friendly element it cannot normally pass through; any corner would pass through an enemy element; any corner would pass through a ZOC being avoided; any corner would pass through impassable terrain; any corner would pass through bad going being avoided.

The distances of intermediate moves are summed for the total movement distance. Any number of intermediate moves may be made as long as the total does not exceed the maximum.

---

## Group Moves

Two or more elements may make a group move observing the following restrictions:

- No element may exceed its maximum movement distance.
- Elements must begin and end in group contact.
- No element may change position relative to any other element.
- The group may move directly forward, wheel, or a combination including multiple wheels.
- The group may move up to one-half base width directly left or right to line up opposite an enemy element (only if that enemy is within one base width of at least one element in the group).
- No element may start in or enter bad going unless moving as a column (exception: a group composed entirely of Psiloi).
- No element may start in or enter a river unless moving as a column.

### Group Contact

Two or more elements are in group contact if:
- All elements are facing the same direction.
- Every element is in edge-to-edge and corner-to-corner contact with at least one other element.
- The group is contiguous.

### Wheeling

Wheeling occurs on the outside front corner of the leftmost or rightmost element of the front rank.

### Reducing Frontage

A group may reduce its frontage to pass through a gap. A gap is the space between any two of: element (friendly or enemy), camp, terrain piece, or board edge. The gap must be at least one element wide. Elements outside the gap fall in behind those that pass through.

### Column Moves

Two or more elements may move as a column:
- Must begin in column contact (one element wide).
- No element may change order relative to any other.
- No element may exceed its maximum movement distance.
- The column may move directly forward or the lead element may wheel (with all others following).

### Forming Column

Elements in group contact may form a column as a column move. Designate a lead element; all others move freely to fall in behind. No element may move backwards to join the column; all must end in the one-element-wide column.

---

## Littoral Landings

A player who has reserved elements for a littoral landing may, in their first turn, place any or all of the reserved elements onto the game board. The elements must be placed in group contact. At least two elements must be in contact with the edge of the waterway. Placing a littoral landing costs **1 PIP**.

Elements not placed in the first turn may not be used at all during the game, but do not count as destroyed.

---

## Second and Subsequent Movement

| Element Type | How Many Moves | Conditions |
|-------------|---------------|------------|
| Psiloi | Unlimited | Only in the player's first turn. |
| Warband | 1 additional move | Must end second move in full front/flank/rear contact with enemy, or eligible to provide rear support, or eligible for overlap. |
| Light Horse, Light Camelry | Unlimited | May not start, move, or end within one base width of an enemy element or enemy occupied camp during a second or subsequent move. |
| All | Unlimited | Second and subsequent moves must be road moves and may not end in contact with an enemy element or enemy occupied camp. |

Each second or subsequent move costs PIPs in the normal way.

---

## Moving Through Friendly Elements

### Passing Through a Friendly Element

An element may pass through one or more friendly elements if:
- Every met element is facing the same or opposite direction as the moving element.
- There is clear space immediately beyond the farthest met element large enough to hold the moving element AND the moving element may reach this space.
- Mounted elements may move through psiloi, and psiloi may move through any friendly element. Other elements may not pass through friendly elements.

### Cutting the Corner

An element can also move through a friendly element if only one of the lines drawn between the starting and ending positions of each corner passes through a friendly element. This is open to all element types.

---

## Breaking Off from Full Front Contact

An element in full front contact with an enemy element or enemy occupied camp may only move by breaking off (always a single element move). Restrictions:

- Its tactical move distance must be greater than the enemy element's in the terrain it ends its move in.
- It may only move directly to its rear. No change of direction or facing.
- It must move at least **3 MU**.
- It may not start with an enemy element in full flank or full rear contact.
- It may not pass through a friendly element unless normally allowed.
- It may not pass through an enemy element or enemy controlled camp.

If an element breaks off from Knights, Scythed Chariots, Warband, or Elephants, the element that was broken off from **immediately pursues** as an outcome move.

---

## Zone of Control

Every element exerts a **zone of control (ZOC)** that restricts the movement of enemy elements.

The ZOC extends out **one base width** directly in front of the element, or until blocked by another element (whichever is less). An element that blocks a ZOC is itself considered to be within the ZOC. The ZOC includes the front edge of the ZOC region but not the side edges or front corners.

### Blocking a ZOC

Use the "rolling carpet" method: the ZOC extends out from the element until it hits another element or an occupied camp, stopping at that point across its whole width.

### Starting a Tactical Move Within an Enemy ZOC

An element may:
- **Retire** — move directly to its rear, no change of direction or facing.
- **Move to full front contact** with any one controlling enemy element.
- **Square itself** with any one controlling enemy element (following specific angular and directional restrictions).

### Entering an Enemy ZOC During a Tactical Move

The element may move normally until it first touches the ZOC, then may:
- Move to full front contact with any one controlling enemy element.
- Square itself with any one controlling enemy element.

### War Wagons and ZOC

- War Wagon elements normally exert a ZOC from **all four edges**.
- If an enemy element is in full front contact with a War Wagon, that War Wagon does **not** exert a ZOC from **any** edge.

---

## Full Front, Flank, and Rear Contact

**Full Front Contact:** Mutual front edge to front edge and front corner to front corner contact. An element is in full front contact with an enemy occupied camp if any part of its front edge is in contact with any edge of the camp.

**Full Flank Contact:** Front edge in contact with the enemy's side edge AND mutual left-to-left or right-to-right front corner contact. An element cannot be in full flank contact with a camp.

**Full Rear Contact:** Front edge in contact with the enemy's rear edge AND front corner-to-rear corner contact. An element cannot be in full rear contact with a camp.

---

## Moving into Contact

Artillery and War Wagons may **not** move into contact with an enemy element or enemy occupied camp (including overlap position).

All other element types may move into contact. Unless the contacted element is forced to conform, the moving element or group must maneuver to reach a full contact position to initiate close combat.

### Flank and Rear Contact

**Flank Contact:** An element may not end in full flank contact unless it starts entirely beyond a line prolonging the enemy element's side edge, or partly beyond the side edge and partly behind the rear edge.

**Rear Contact:** An element may not end in full rear contact unless it starts entirely behind a line prolonging the enemy element's rear edge.

### Conforming to a Group

Any single element contacted by an enemy element must conform to that element if all of the following apply:
- The enemy element is moving as part of a group move (or littoral landing with 2+ elements).
- The contacted element is not in group contact with one or more friendly elements.
- There is sufficient clear space to conform.
- There will be at least one base depth of unoccupied space behind the element after it conforms.

To conform, the element immediately turns to face the contacting element, ending in full front contact.

### Turning to Face

At the end of the tactical movement phase, any element with an enemy element in full flank or full rear contact (but no element in full front contact) immediately turns to face the contacting element.

**War Wagons** do not turn to face. Instead, the first edge contacted is treated as the front edge for close combat purposes.

## Distant Shooting

Distant shooting represents long-range shooting of massed bowmen, war wagons, and artillery. All short-range shooting is included in close combat.

Eligible shooters: **Bows, Pavises, War Wagons, Artillery.**

- Bows, Pavises, and War Wagons shoot in both their own turn and the opponent's turn.
- Artillery normally only shoots in their own turn if they did not make a tactical move. However, if Artillery are shot at, they may also shoot (return fire).

| Element Type | Maximum Range |
|-------------|--------------|
| Bows, Pavises, War Wagons | 3 MU |
| Artillery | 8 MU |

The active player chooses the order of shooting. Each combat is fully resolved before the next. An element may not shoot more than once per turn. An element may not be shot at more than once per turn (though up to three shooters may combine against a target, with the closest as the main shooter).

Elements eligible to shoot **must** do so. Elements eligible to shoot at one another **must** do so.

An element that shoots without being shot at ignores any combat result that would affect it.

### Eligibility

An element is eligible to shoot if:
- At least part of the target edge is in the arc of fire of the shooting element.
- The distance from any part of the shooting edge to any part of the target edge is ≤ the maximum range.
- The path from the shooting edge to the target edge is not blocked.
- Neither the shooting element nor the target element is in full front, flank, or rear contact with an enemy element.

Elements in overlap may shoot and be shot at.

### Arc of Fire

- For Bows, Pavises, and Artillery: a rectangular area extending forward from the front edge, one base width to either side.
- For War Wagons: a full 360° circle.
- For Bows, Pavises, or Artillery occupying a camp: a full 360° circle.

### Blocked Path

The path from shooting edge to target edge is blocked if:
- Any part of any element is within the path area (element whose edge runs along the path lines does not block).
- The target element is entirely in a woods or oasis.
- The shooting element is entirely in a woods or oasis.
- The entire width of the shooting path is blocked by the crest of a gentle or steep hill.
- The entire width of the shooting path is blocked by a woods or oasis.

### Shooting on a Rear Edge

An element is eligible to shoot exclusively on a rear edge if:
- The shooting edge is entirely behind an imaginary line running along the rear edge of the target.
- The element is allowed to shoot at the rear edge of the target.

If aided by extra shooters, all must be eligible to shoot exclusively on the rear edge, otherwise it counts as a regular shot.

---

## Close Combat

Close combat represents all fighting at close quarters: skirmishers, horse archers, missile-throwing foot troops, and hand-to-hand combat.

All pairs of elements in mutual full front contact at the beginning of the close combat phase participate. The active player chooses the order of combat. Each combat is fully resolved before the next.

### Uphill

An element is uphill of its opponent if at least some part of its front edge is on a gentle or steep hill AND part of its front edge is uphill of its opponent's entire front edge.

### Overlap

An element overlaps by **corner contact** if:
- Not in full flank contact with the enemy.
- In mutual left-to-left or right-to-right front corner contact.
- A friendly element is in full front contact with the enemy element.
- Not in full front contact with another enemy element.

An element overlaps by **side contact** if:
- In mutual side edge-to-side edge contact.
- A friendly element is in full front contact with the enemy element.

An element may receive at most: one -1 for overlap/full flank contact on the left, one -1 on the right, and one -1 for full rear contact.

### Attacking a Camp

An element in full front contact with an enemy occupied camp may participate in close combat. Only **one element** may attack a camp per close combat phase. If the defending element is destroyed, the attacking element immediately occupies the camp.

### Rear Support

**Pikes:** Receive rear support from a second rank of Pikes directly behind (both entirely in good going).
- +3 vs all Foot except Bows and Psiloi
- +2 vs Knights, Cataphracts, and Elephants

**Light Spears, Spears, Warband:** Receive +1 rear support from a second rank of the same type directly behind (both entirely in good going), vs all Foot except Bows and Psiloi, and vs Knights, Cataphracts, and Elephants.

**Psiloi support for Auxilia, Blades, Light Spears, Raiders, Spears:** +1 when fighting Mounted, Warband, or a camp. Psiloi must be directly behind the supported element OR directly behind an element of the same type that is in side edge and front corner contact with the supported element. Psiloi provide support even when in bad going. A psiloi element may support more than one element in the same turn.

> Note that Light Spears are distinct from Spears and may not be mixed for rear support. Likewise, Raiders are distinct from Blades.

---

## Combat Resolution

Combat resolution is the same for both distant shooting and close combat:

1. Calculate the combat, tactical, and rear support factors for both elements.
2. Roll a die for each element (combat die roll).
3. Add all factors to the die roll → **combat score**.
4. Compare the two combat scores and perform any outcome moves from the combat results table.

> Tip: Factors are calculated at the time of each combat based on the current disposition of elements. The result of one combat frequently affects surrounding combats, making the order of resolution very important.

## Outcome Movement

Outcome movement includes all actions that occur as a result of combat. Apply to the losing element; winning elements may need to pursue.

**Full Flank or Full Rear Contact:** If an element recoils, flees, or is destroyed while in full front contact with an enemy element, any friendly element in full flank or full rear contact with that enemy element must recoil.

**Supporting Elements** are not affected if the element they supported is destroyed.

### Recoil

An element moves directly to its rear (without turning) its own base depth or one base width, whichever is less.

When recoiling from distant shooting where all shooting edges are entirely behind a line prolonging the target element's rear edge, the target element first turns 180° in place, then recoils.

An element that must recoil is **immediately destroyed** if it:
- Starts with an enemy element in full flank or full rear contact
- Meets an enemy element
- Meets a camp
- Meets a friendly element it cannot pass through or push back
- Is forced to recoil while occupying a camp
- Meets impassable terrain
- Meets the edge of the board

A recoiling element **destroys** an enemy element it meets if:
- Its rear edge or rear corner meets the enemy's rear edge
- Its rear edge meets the enemy's rear corner
- Its rear corner only meets the enemy's side edge

A recoiling elephant that meets a friendly element destroys that element. Two elephants recoiling into each other are both destroyed.

### Pass Through

A recoiling element passes through a friendly element if:
- The met element faces the same direction
- There is clear space immediately beyond large enough to hold the recoiling element
- The combination is allowed (Mounted → any except Pikes/Elephants; Blades → Blades/Spears; Pikes → Blades; Bows → Blades; Psiloi → any except Psiloi)

### Push Back

A recoiling element pushes a friendly element back if:
- It cannot pass through the met element
- The met element faces the same direction
- The met element is not an elephant or war wagon

Both elements move to the rear until the recoil is complete. If the pushed element meets another element, it pushes it back if it can; otherwise the recoiling element is destroyed. Cascading push-back is allowed for friendly elements facing the same direction that are not elephants or war wagons.

### Flee

To flee:
1. Recoil
2. Turn 180° in place
3. Make the equivalent of a full tactical move in the new direction

The move may only change direction by the minimum necessary to avoid: enemy elements, friendly elements it may not pass through, an occupied camp, impassable terrain, and the following bad going:

| Fleeing Element | Avoids |
|----------------|--------|
| Cavalry | Woods, Steep Hills, Oasis, Dunes |
| Camelry | Woods, Steep Hills |
| Light Camelry | (same as Camelry) |
| Psiloi | None |
| Light Horse | None |

A fleeing element ignores enemy ZOCs. A fleeing element is destroyed if it meets the edge of the board or a river. A fleeing element halts if it cannot move or continue its move.

### Pursuit

Elements of **Knights, Scythed Chariots, Elephants, or Warband** must immediately pursue if in full front contact with an enemy element that recoils, flees, is destroyed, or breaks off.

When Warband pursues, any element of Warband that provided rear support (or is eligible to provide support against a breaking-off element) also pursues.

To pursue, an element moves directly forward its own base depth or one base width (whichever is less). The pursuing element stops if it meets another element, an occupied camp, impassable terrain, or a board edge.

Pursuit and bad going:
- Knights and Scythed Chariots pursue into Marsh and Rough; stop at edge of other bad going.
- Elephants and Warband pursue into all bad going.

An element occupying a camp does not pursue. If the pursuing element meets an unoccupied camp, it occupies it.

## Examples: Distant Shooting

### Example 1

Bow A shoots at Knights, aided by Bow B.

- Bow A factors: Bows vs. Mounted = +4; no tactical factor → **total: +4**
- Knights factors: Knights vs. Foot = +3; extra shooter -1 → **total: +2**

Bow A rolls 3 + 4 = 7. Knights roll 6 + 2 = 8. Bow A is beaten, but since it shoots without being shot at, it ignores the result.

### Example 2

Bow A shoots at Bow X, aided by Bow B.

- Bow A factors: Bows vs. Foot = +2; no tactical factor → **total: +2**
- Bow X factors: Bows vs. Foot = +2; extra shooter -1 → **total: +1**

Bow X rolls 2 + 1 = 3. Bow A rolls 5 + 2 = 7. Bow X is doubled and is destroyed.

---

## Examples: Close Combat

### Example 1

Knights in mutual front contact with Spear A. Spear B overlaps the Knights.

- Knights factors: Knights vs. Foot = +3; overlapped -1 → **total: +2**
- Spear A factors: Spears vs. Mounted = +4; no tactical/rear support → **total: +4**

Knights roll 3 + 2 = 5. Spear A rolls 4 + 4 = 8. Knights are beaten but not doubled → recoil. Spear A does not pursue.

### Example 2

Knights in mutual front contact with Spears. Psiloi provide rear support to Spears.

- Knights factors: Knights vs. Foot = +3 → **total: +3**
- Spears factors: Spears vs. Mounted = +4; Psiloi rear support +1 → **total: +5**

Knights roll 5 + 3 = 8. Spears roll 2 + 5 = 7. Spears are beaten but not doubled. Spears are destroyed (vs. Knights in good going). The Psiloi are not affected. Knights immediately pursue, moving forward into contact with the Psiloi.

## Winning and Losing

> *To crush your enemies, to see them driven before you, and to hear the lamentations of their women!*  
> — Attributed to Ghengis Khan, and later to Conan the Barbarian

A player wins when, **at the end of a turn**, both of the following conditions are met:
- Either the opposing general's element OR at least **four** of the opposing army's troop elements have been destroyed.
- The opposing army has lost **more elements** than the player's army.

Special counting rules:
- Scythed chariot elements and camp follower elements are **not** counted as destroyed elements.
- A camp last occupied by an enemy element counts as **two elements** destroyed.

## Glossary

**Aggression** — Measure of how likely an army was to fight outside its home region. Used in determining invader and defender.

**Army List** — Description of an army including start/end dates, enemies, allies, home topography, aggression, and element types.

**Bad Going** — Terrain that impedes movement and combat.

**Barker Marker** — Another name for a ZOC marker. Named after the main author of DBA.

**Camp** — Playing piece representing the baggage, tents, and livestock that follow an army on campaign.

**Close Combat** — All fighting at close quarters, including close-range missile fire and hand-to-hand combat.

**Closing the Door** — A move where an element goes from overlapping an enemy element to full flank contact with that element.

**Defender** — Army on the strategic defensive. Chooses terrain, sets up first, moves second.

**Deployment Zone** — The area on the game board where an army's troop elements may be deployed.

**Dismount** — To exchange a mounted element with its foot equivalent. Only allowed when specified by the army list.

**Distant Shooting** — Long-range shooting of massed bowmen (Bows and Pavises), War Wagons, and Artillery.

**Element** — Basic playing piece of the game. Each army is made up of twelve elements.

**General** — Element designated to include the leader of the army.

**Good Going** — Open ground that doesn't impede movement or combat. Any part of the board without a terrain piece is good going. Gentle hills are also good going.

**Group** — Two or more elements in edge and corner contact, eligible to move together.

**Impetuous** — Term used to describe element types that pursue (Knights, Warbands, Scythed Chariots, and Elephants).

**Invader** — Army on the strategic offensive. Rolls for base edge, sets up second, moves first.

**Jackknife** — Having elements in both full front and full flank contact with an enemy element.

**Littoral Landing** — Special move for armies with Littoral home topography. 0–4 elements reserved during deployment and placed along a waterway in the first turn.

**Move Stick** — A small stick marked in 1 MU increments. Used to measure movement and distances.

**Outcome Move** — Involuntary movement of an element due to a combat result.

**Overlap** — An element that hangs over the end of the enemy line or is in side contact with an enemy element. Provides an advantage in close combat.

**PIPs** — Player Initiative Points. Rolled at the beginning of a turn and spent to move elements.

**Quick Kill** — A situation where an element is destroyed if it is beaten in combat.

**Tactical Move** — Voluntary movement of one or more elements.

**Wheel Stick** — A small stick marked in base width (40mm or 60mm) increments. Assists in wheeling groups of elements.

**ZOC** — Zone of control. The area directly in front of an element, up to one base width away.

**ZOC Marker** — A square piece of wood or metal, one base width on a side, used to determine if an element is within another's ZOC.
